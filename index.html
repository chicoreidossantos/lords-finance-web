How it works

Three tokens and one reserve of ETH.

Solidus (SOLIDUS) The dollar

Anyone can mint SOLIDUS with ETH and redeem it for ETH, at close to one dollar, without asking anyone’s permission. The reserve holds only ETH, part of it staked through Lido.

Orders settle in batches, at the next ETH price published by Chainlink, the protocol’s only price source.

What you earn: a small share of the protocol’s income, added to your balance, and DUKES while you hold it. It is meant to stay worth about one dollar.

Florins (FLOR) The Lords’ capital

Lords put ETH into the reserve and receive Florins. This capital stands beneath SOLIDUS and absorbs the losses.

What you earn: most of the protocol’s income (the staking yield on the reserve and the fees), and DUKES. It is a leveraged position in ETH: you gain more when ETH rises, and you can lose everything when it falls.

Dukes (DUKES) The reward token

A reward token with a fixed supply, set at deployment. Half goes to participants, SOLIDUS holders and Lords, over time; half goes to the team.

What you earn: a fixed share of the protocol’s income, paid in Florins, as long as the protocol exists. No vote and no other right.

Series

Each series of SOLIDUS carries the name of a historical coin. If the reserve falls too close to what SOLIDUS holders are owed, the series stops: its holders are paid in ETH, less than a dollar each. A new series then opens under the next name.

Why it holds

The idea behind the design, without the mathematics.

“If men were angels, no government would be necessary.”

James Madison, Federalist No. 51, 1788
The Madisonian principle

Rules are written for self-interested agents, not for angels. The system is stable not in spite of people trying to profit from it, but because of it: every profitable move should push coverage away from the trigger. A rule that needs good behavior to work is a weakness.

The system pays opportunists to keep it stable.

Coverage

Coverage = value of the reserve ÷ SOLIDUS in circulation. Minting lowers it, so the lower it is, the harder minting gets. No voluntary operation can take it below 1.3; only a fall in the value of the reserve can.

  • 1.2 or lessThe trigger. The series stops, in the orderly way described below.
  • 1.2 to 1.3Ordinary minting closed. Only the combined mint, which brings at least 2 dollars of ETH per SOLIDUS. Redeeming stays open.
  • 1.3 to 2Minting with a one-hour wait. A mint settles at the next price after an hour, and never takes coverage below 1.3.
  • 2 or moreFast minting. A mint settles in the next batch, as far as coverage stays at 2 or more; the rest waits an hour.
  • About 1.45 to 1.55Default automatic exits. A wallet that never chose a level is redeemed when coverage, counting pending mints, reaches its own level in this band. Holders can change it or turn it off.

How opportunists stabilize it

  • SOLIDUS above a dollar

    Minting at 1.001 and selling above that pays, and the selling brings the price down. During the 90-day cap, minting stops when the cap is full, so this works only in part.

    Price falls
  • SOLIDUS below a dollar

    Buying below 0.999 and redeeming at 0.999 pays, and the buying brings the price up. Each redemption also raises coverage.

    Price rises
  • Coverage falls

    The Lords’ capital becomes a smaller part of the reserve and still takes most of its income, so entering as a Lord at book value becomes more attractive. Each entry adds ETH and no SOLIDUS.

    Coverage rises
  • Coverage below 1.3

    Whoever wants SOLIDUS must bring at least 2 dollars of ETH for each one, and takes the part above the SOLIDUS price in Florins.

    Coverage rises
  • Running the protocol

    Anyone can run the batches and the automatic exits, and collects a fee for doing it. The protocol needs no operator.

    No one in charge

What limits the loss

If ETH falls far enough for coverage to reach 1.2, the series stops in an orderly way. Each SOLIDUS of that series becomes a claim on a fixed amount of ETH, worth 0.95 dollars at the stop price, paid from a pool set aside for its holders. If the reserve is worth less than that, holders share all of it. The rest stays with the Lords, and a new series opens under the next name.

There are 20 series. If the 20th stops, the protocol ends.

Nobody can change the rules

No administrator, no owner, no pause and no vote. The contracts cannot be modified, and no function changes a parameter.

One price source: Chainlink. The protocol depends on it and does not control it.
