In development

A dollar backed only by ETH.

No custodian, no admin keys, no governance. The contracts are immutable: once deployed, nobody can change them, and nobody can freeze a balance.

An AI-created decentralized stablecoin: designed, specified and coded by a team of AI agents; a human decides and deploys it.

How it works

One reserve of ETH and staked ETH, a dollar token and its wrapped version, the Lords’ capital, and a reward token.

Solidus (SOLIDUS) The dollar

Anyone can mint SOLIDUS with ETH at $1.001 and redeem it for ETH and wstETH at $0.999, without asking anyone’s permission. Orders settle once a day, in a batch. The reserve holds only ETH and wstETH, ETH staked through Lido.

Minting opens in batch 31: the first 30 batches take only Lords’ entries. After that, minting is open while coverage stays at 2 or more. Redemptions are always open: at $0.999; at $0.99 when coverage is below 1.3; and at $0.99 × coverage when coverage is below 1, so $0.99 is not a floor.

What you receive: your balance is rebalanced each day to pass on SOLIDUS’s part of the reserve’s income. This is a rebalancing, not interest: it depends on the income of the day, it is not promised, and the balance can also go down in a conversion.

Wrapped Solidus (wSOLIDUS) Fixed balance

A wrapper with a fixed balance, like wstETH: instead of the balance changing, each wSOLIDUS is worth more SOLIDUS over time. It is made for pools and lending, where tokens whose balance changes work badly. It has no exit box.

After a conversion, wSOLIDUS sells the ETH it received for USDC, in a CoW auction, and buys SOLIDUS at $1.01 or less until it is again all SOLIDUS.

Unlike SOLIDUS, wSOLIDUS may at times hold centralized stablecoins.

Florins (FLOR) The Lords’ capital

Lords put ETH into the reserve at book value and receive Florins. This capital, the reserve minus the SOLIDUS owed, stands beneath SOLIDUS and takes the falls of ETH first.

What you receive: the Lords’ part of the reserve’s income, and the DUKES for participants. It is a leveraged position in ETH, coverage ÷ (coverage − 1) times: you gain more when ETH rises, and you can lose everything when it falls. Below coverage 2, exits are limited.

Dukes (DUKES) The reward token

A fixed supply of 100,000,000, created at deployment. Half goes to the Lords over time; half goes to the team, released over four years. Of the team’s half, the team commits 3,000,000 to bug rewards and 1,000,000 to the testnet; that split is a public commitment, not code.

What you receive: DUKES holders share 10% of the protocol’s income in each batch that opens with coverage above 1. At coverage 1 or below, that 10% is not paid: it stays in the reserve. No vote and no other right. Their value depends on the size of the system.

The daily batch

The protocol has one clock: a batch a day. Every order of the day settles together, at one price.

Cutoff at 00:00 UTC

Orders placed during the day wait for the cutoff at 00:00 UTC. The app shows a countdown. An order can be cancelled until the cutoff, or until a valid Chainlink round after the cutoff exists.

The batch settles at the first valid Chainlink ETH/USD price round observed after the cutoff. Once that round exists, the batch price is fixed and orders can no longer be cancelled, even if nobody has processed the batch yet. If no round comes, the orders move to the next batch, and they can still be cancelled.

One coverage for the whole batch

Coverage is measured once, at the batch price, after the Lords’ entries and the combined orders. That opening coverage sets the redemption price, the exit box, the conversion, the coupon and the sharing of the income for the whole batch.

Each order pays its own processing fee, to whoever processes the batch. Anyone can do it; the protocol needs no operator and pays no reward.

The order inside a batch

  1. Lords’ entries and combined orders. Then the opening coverage is measured.
  2. Exit boxes.
  3. Redemptions, and Lords’ exits matched against them.
  4. Conversion, at most one per batch, only with coverage at or below 1.2.
  5. Mints, the exit box’s automatic return and Lords’ net withdrawals. These are the only steps that lower coverage: if they do not all fit while keeping coverage at 2 or more, they are filled in proportion, up to coverage 2.

Why it holds

The idea behind the design, without the mathematics.

“If men were angels, no government would be necessary.”

James Madison, Federalist No. 51, 1788
The Madisonian principle

Rules are written for self-interested agents, not for angels. Every rule assumes each participant is a bot that takes every profitable action. The system is stable not in spite of people trying to profit from it, but because of it. A rule that needs good behavior to work is a weakness.

The system pays opportunists to keep it stable.

Coverage = value of the reserve ÷ SOLIDUS in circulation. Minting lowers it, so minting is open only while it stays at 2 or more. Below 2, the only way to mint brings more ETH than the SOLIDUS it creates.

  • SOLIDUS above a dollar

    Minting at $1.001 and selling above that pays, and the selling brings the price down. Minting stops when coverage would fall below 2, and during the 90-batch cap when the cap is full, so this works only in part.

  • SOLIDUS below a dollar

    Buying below $0.999 and redeeming at $0.999 pays, and the buying brings the price up. Each redemption also raises coverage.

  • Coverage below 2

  • Minting below 2

    Whoever wants new SOLIDUS must bring at least 2 dollars of ETH for each one, and takes the part above $1.001 in Florins.

  • Running the protocol

What limits the loss

If ETH falls far enough for coverage to open a batch at 1.2 or less, one third of all SOLIDUS is converted into ETH at $0.99 per SOLIDUS, in proportion to each balance. At most one conversion per batch. There is no stop and no closing: the system goes on, smaller.

Below coverage 1, the conversion pays $0.99 times coverage. The Lords’ capital is then at zero.

Nobody can change the rules

No administrator, no owner, no pause and no vote. The contracts cannot be modified, and no function changes a parameter. A bug cannot be fixed either: only the testnet and the bug rewards stand against it.

One price source: Chainlink. The protocol depends on it and does not control it.

The exit box

An optional way for a SOLIDUS holder to leave before a conversion, and to come back by itself.

How it works

Checking the exit box deposits your SOLIDUS in a single exit box contract, outside the core. It keeps receiving the daily rebalancing. There is one level for everyone: 1.5.

In the first batch that opens with coverage below 1.5, all the SOLIDUS in the box leaves together. The core pays $1.00 per SOLIDUS in ETH and wstETH at the batch price, even if a jump of ETH has taken coverage below 1.3. Only in a batch that opens below coverage 1 does it pay less: coverage × $1.00. Each address chooses what happens next with the “Convert to USDT” option, checked by default in the app. With it, the contract makes a single sale to USDT and credits each address its share. Without it, nothing is sold: your share stays in ETH and wstETH. You withdraw your share whenever you want; what you withdraw does not come back by itself.

$1.00 in ETH, less the cost of the sale. Without the sale: $1.00 in ETH and wstETH.

The automatic return

With the sale to USDT: in the first batch with coverage at 2 or more, the contract buys ETH with the USDT nobody withdrew, and the mint back at $1.000 joins the following batch, one batch later, filled in proportion with the other mints up to coverage 2. The contract buys ETH only for its share of the room; the rest stays in USDT and tries again in the next batch.

Without the sale: the ETH and wstETH themselves are minted back at $1.000, nothing is bought, in the first batch with room, filled in proportion up to coverage 2. You get back as many SOLIDUS as that ETH is worth then: more if ETH rose while you were out, fewer if it fell. What comes back, either way, is armed again. The return pays no fee to the reserve.

The fee is fixed: the gas, shared by amount, plus a small margin, capped at 1%. Without the sale there is less gas: only leaving and coming back. The gas does not depend on how many addresses there are.

The app checks the box by default when you mint, with “Convert to USDT” checked, and offers it the same way if you connect with SOLIDUS outside it; that needs your own transaction. You can uncheck “Convert to USDT” then or later. The contract itself does not check it, and other wallets and exchanges do not either. It cannot be checked in a batch that opens below 1.5.

  • What it costs. The exit box is the price of peace of mind, and it is not free. In the team’s simulations, in the middle scenario, holding SOLIDUS with the box and the sale to USDT costs about 0.8% a year, against less than 0.1% a year without the box. Most of it is the rebalancing you lose while out and the slippage of each exit and return. In exchange, SOLIDUS in the box is never converted: it leaves first. The figures assume a slippage of 0.2% on the sale and 0.2% on the purchase back, and bots that hedge their ETH at no cost; they change with the real ones.
  • With the sale, while you are out you hold USDT, a centralized stablecoin that its issuer can freeze, right during the crisis, until the batch after coverage is back at 2. Without it, you hold ETH and wstETH: what you have in dollars goes up and down with ETH, right when ETH is falling, until it comes back. Either way, you do not receive the rebalancing while you are out.
  • The sale of all the boxes with “Convert to USDT” at once is large. Its slippage is paid by those holders.
  • A jump of ETH within a single day can take coverage from above 1.5 to below 1.3 between two batches. The box still pays $1.00. It pays less only in a batch that opens below coverage 1: coverage × $1.00.

Lords and Florins

The Lords put up the capital at risk. What they receive, and how they come in and go out.

Their part of the income

The reserve’s income, the staking rewards and the mint and redeem fees, enters the reserve. 10% goes to DUKES holders, except at coverage 1 or below, when it stays in the reserve. With coverage at 2 or more, the rest is shared in proportion to capital: SOLIDUS holders by the rebalancing, Lords through the book value of the Florin.

Below coverage 2, the Lords’ part rises in a straight line from 50% at 2 to 80% at 1.3, and stays at 80% below. The cent that stays in the reserve in each redemption or conversion at $0.99 also goes to the Lords.

The Lords pay for the exit box: each SOLIDUS that leaves through it gets $1.00 instead of $0.999, or instead of $0.99 after a jump below coverage 1.3, and comes back at $1.000 instead of $1.001. Bots use the box too, without the sale to USDT.

The DUKES for participants go only to Florins, by capital. A batch that opens with coverage 1 or below hands out none.

Coming in

At book value, in any batch. Below coverage 2, a new Lord chooses between a plain entry and an entry with a coupon.

The coupon is not transferable. It is worth 3% × (2 − coverage) ÷ 0.7 of the entry, up to 3% at coverage 1.3 or below, and it turns into Florins when coverage is back at 2. If the address withdraws or sells Florins before that, the coupon shrinks in proportion.

Below coverage 1, nobody can enter.

Going out

  • Coverage 2 or more: net withdrawal in ETH and wstETH, at book value less the 0.1% band on the SOLIDUS behind your Florins, as far as coverage stays at 2 or more after the batch.
  • Below 2: only exits that do not lower coverage. A proportional exit, handing in the same fraction of all SOLIDUS as of all Florins, your own SOLIDUS or SOLIDUS bought with one button; it is immediate and needs no price. An exit matched against the redemptions of the same batch. Or a sale of Florins in a FLOR/ETH pool, outside the core, if one exists.

    Medieval Monopoly Money Phase

    Before mainnet, a public testnet with play money. Exploiting flaws is allowed, and the players who gain the most receive prizes in DUKES.

  3. 2027

    Mainnet, with a cap

    Deployment on Ethereum, with limits on the amounts for the first 90 daily batches. The first 30 batches are for Florins only: Lords can enter and exit, and minting SOLIDUS, combined orders and net withdrawals open in batch 31. The DUKES for participants start with the first batch.

  4. 2027

    Stablecoin

    The cap ends after 90 batches. From then on, anyone can mint and redeem SOLIDUS at close to one dollar, in any amount the coverage allows.

Before you use it

  • The price comes later. An order does not settle when you place it. It waits for the cutoff at 00:00 UTC and for the first valid Chainlink round after it, and then settles at that price. You can cancel it until the cutoff, or until a valid Chainlink round after the cutoff exists. During the wait you stay exposed to ETH. If no round comes, the order moves to the next batch.
  • One price source. Chainlink ETH/USD is the protocol’s only price, with nothing to check it against. A wrong round settles the whole batch at that price and can set off a conversion. The feed’s owners can change it without notice.
  • Paid in ETH and wstETH. The protocol pays in ETH and wstETH, in the proportions of the reserve, and the payment can include receipts from Lido’s withdrawal queue, which take days, or weeks, to collect. wstETH can sell for less than ETH.
  • Lido. Part of the reserve is wstETH. A failure of wstETH hits the reserve.
  • No yield is promised. The rebalancing depends on the reserve’s income and can be zero. A conversion pays $0.99 in ETH and wstETH, or $0.99 × coverage below coverage 1.
  • Immutable code. A bug cannot be fixed.
